# Project Flint 0.1.0-alpha.16 — Primitive Campfire, Multitool, and Earthworks

## Intent

Alpha.16 turns the primitive opening into a more coherent material chain after the alpha.15 interaction/stability pass. The goals are to make Sugar Cane/cutters behavior intuitive, introduce a persistent relightable fire without invalidating the earlier disposable fire, open the first true leaf/earth/gravel operations through a Crude Multitool, and keep all of these processes physical and readable rather than moving progression into a crafting grid.

Only the requested progression/bug-fix batch is changed. Alpha.15 pickup, Creeper stability, advancement layout philosophy, five-slot inventory, worldgen, animal behavior, hunger, and accepted earlier timings remain in place unless directly involved below.

## Player-facing behavior

### Rendering fix

Placed Plant Tool Bindings in the Crude Stone Cutters ground assembly no longer resolve to a missing/null texture. The multipart assembly now references a dedicated block-atlas binding texture.

### Twigs and cutters

- Refined Rock forage-bush harvest: **3–4 Twigs**.
- Crude Stone Cutters forage-bush harvest: **4 Twigs guaranteed**, costing 1 cutter durability.
- Cutters now harvest Sugar Cane into the normal amount of **vanilla Sugar Cane**, including the cane column above the targeted block, rather than automatically converting the plant into Crude Reeds.
- The existing **Crude Stone Hammer → Sugar Cane → Crude Reeds** operation is intentionally retained as a bootstrap route, because Plant Tool Binding requires Crude Reeds before the first Cutters can exist.
- Deliberate reed processing is separate: **Sugar Cane in main hand + Crude Stone Cutters in offhand**, worked for **8 strikes**, consumes **1 Sugar Cane + 1 cutter durability**, and yields **3 Crude Reeds**.

### Campfire Stone and permanent fire

- **Very Refined Stone in both hands** starts Campfire Stone working.
- It takes **16 strikes**, exactly twice the normal 8-strike Rock knapping process.
- Success chance is **100%**.
- The main-hand Very Refined Stone becomes **1 Campfire Stone**; the offhand Very Refined Stone is the retained working stone.
- Placing a Campfire Stone begins a supported primitive campfire frame. Build it to **12 Campfire Stones + 12 Burnt Twigs**.
- To ignite a complete frame, hold **exactly 8 Flower Petals in the main hand** and **at least 4 Twigs in the offhand**.
- Ignition consumes **only the 8 Flower Petals**. The offhand Twigs are retained.
- The fire remains lit for **30 seconds (600 ticks)**.
- When the fire expires, the assembled frame remains in the world and can be relit indefinitely.

### Burnt materials

The earlier disposable 12-Twig Pile remains the bootstrap fire source. Its existing 10-second successful flame now converts **Twig → Burnt Twig** rather than directly producing Lightly Burnt Twig. This is required so the player can acquire the 12 Burnt Twigs needed for the permanent frame without introducing an unrelated recipe.

A lit permanent campfire performs:

- **1 Twig → 1 Burnt Twig**;
- **1 Branch → 1 Burnt Branch**.

Burnt Twig has a deliberate post-process:

- **Burnt Twig in main hand + Crude Stone Hammer in offhand**;
- 8 strikes;
- consumes 1 Burnt Twig and 1 hammer durability;
- produces **2 Lightly Burnt Twigs**.

This keeps the existing Crude Stone Hammer/Cutters recipes reachable after the Burnt Twig split.

### Crude Multitool

Sneak-right-click the top of a supported block while holding one of the recipe components to begin the physical assembly. The complete arrangement is:

- **2 Burnt Twigs**;
- **4 Plant Tool Bindings**;
- **4 Very Refined Stone**.

Completion produces the **Crude Multitool**. Its initial alpha.16 durability is **96**; this value is provisional tuning because the batch did not specify a durability number and the tool covers three material families.

The multitool operations are:

- any block in vanilla `#minecraft:leaves` → **4 Twigs guaranteed + 1 Branch**, no vanilla leaf drop, 1 durability;
- Dirt or Grass Block → **1 Dirt Pile**, no vanilla block drop, 1 durability, at **25% vanilla destroy progress** (4× slower);
- Gravel → **1 Flint Chip**, no Gravel/vanilla Flint drop, 1 durability, at **25% vanilla destroy progress** (4× slower).

### Dirt and Flint processing

Dirt Piles are physical 1–9 piles. A full **9 Dirt Pile** arrangement struck with the Crude Stone Hammer becomes **1 Loose Dirt**, costing 1 hammer durability. Loose Dirt is a falling block and obeys gravity like Sand/Gravel.

For Flint:

- hold at least **4 Flint Chips** in the main hand and the **Crude Stone Hammer** in the offhand;
- work for 8 strikes;
- consume exactly 4 Flint Chips and 1 hammer durability;
- receive **1 vanilla Flint**.

## Architecture

Alpha.16 grows the existing `StoneWorkingSystem` into a small process enum so multiple two-hand operations share strike tracking, reset behavior, server payloads, and HUD presentation instead of each implementing an unrelated click counter. `PrimitiveProgressPayload` now distinguishes normal Rock working, Campfire Stone working, Reed cutting, Burnt Twig splitting, and Flint knapping.

`PrimitiveCampfireBlock` owns only the permanent frame's component counts, lit state, 600-tick relight cycle, and two explicit heat transformations. It intentionally does not become a general furnace/temperature simulation.

`MultitoolAssemblyBlock` follows the existing physical-assembly pattern but is started only through sneak-right-click placement on supported ground. `DirtPileBlock` follows the reversible physical-pile pattern. `Loose Dirt` uses Minecraft's falling-block behavior directly.

The primitive destroy-progress boundary remains shared: `BlockStateBaseMixin` decides whether/how quickly a capability can progress; `ServerPlayerGameModeMixin` performs the custom completed transformation and suppresses the corresponding vanilla drops.

## Design reasoning

Sugar Cane now has three progression roles without a softlock: the Hammer provides the pre-cutters bootstrap Crude Reeds, harvesting with Cutters gives the recognizable vanilla resource, and intentionally holding that harvested resource with Cutters produces three Crude Reeds per cane. This removes a surprising automatic conversion and makes the second operation an explicit technology.

The permanent campfire does not replace the Twig Pile because doing so would create a progression loop: the permanent campfire requires Burnt Twigs before it exists. The disposable fire therefore becomes the bootstrap source, while the permanent frame becomes the reusable upgrade.

Very Refined Stone working is intentionally slower but reliable. The 16-strike / 100% tradeoff distinguishes deliberate Campfire Stone shaping from the earlier riskier 8-strike / 68% Rock knapping.

Dirt and Gravel use 25% of vanilla destroy progress rather than a fixed arbitrary duration, preserving relative block/tool timing while making the primitive multitool exactly four times slower than the vanilla progress it would otherwise receive.

## Validation

- All Project Flint JSON resources are parsed by `tools/validate_release.py`.
- The validator checks the new registrations, blockstate/model spaces, source texture provenance markers, advancement graph, hand-working constants/routes, permanent campfire requirements, multitool outputs/speeds, Dirt Pile compaction, falling Loose Dirt registration, and Flint reconstruction path.
- The modified Java set passes a Java 21 compile/type check against the cached Minecraft 1.21.11 and remapped Fabric API classes. Compile-only stubs are used solely for artifact-environment dependencies that are absent because the normal Fabric Loom plugin cannot resolve offline; those stubs are not shipped.
- The normal Gradle build remains blocked in this environment because Fabric Loom 1.14.10 is unavailable from the local plugin cache. The included local build remains the final runtime/client release gate.

## Known limitations

- The Crude Multitool's 96 durability is provisional because this batch specified its capabilities but not its durability.
- The permanent campfire currently performs immediate one-item heat transformations while lit; it does not model per-item cook time, fuel consumption, temperature, or queued inventory.
- Campfire flame visuals use block light plus particles rather than a dedicated animated fire model.
- The new hand-working process list is still code-defined. A data-driven processing definition is intentionally deferred until playtesting confirms these process shapes.
- Loose Dirt has gravity but no broader soil-state simulation yet.

## Next release pressure

Playtest the new progression for softlocks and interaction friction, especially Burnt Twig bootstrapping, campfire component recovery, sneak-placement of the multitool assembly, cutters Sugar Cane behavior, and the 4× Dirt/Gravel timings. If these interaction patterns survive playtesting, the next architecture pass should generalize tool operations/processing definitions rather than adding more hard-coded target branches.
