# Project Flint 0.1.0-alpha.11 — Primitive Interaction & Carrying Cleanup

## Intent
Resolve five primitive-opening friction points before adding more technology: Loose Rock gathering depending on an empty hand, wild wolves acquiring players from too far away, full-inventory item entities becoming an unintended carrying exploit, placed Twig Piles being irreversible, and the advancement tree failing to keep pace with newly introduced progression.

This release also corrects the remaining locked-slot render-order problem reported during inventory testing. No unrelated progression timings, item probabilities, animal damage/reach, world generation, or existing stack limits are retuned.

## Player-facing behavior
- Loose Rocks may be gathered while the player is holding another item.
- A Loose Rock is removed from the world only if its Rock item can fit completely inside the five usable primitive inventory slots.
- Ordinary world item entities are picked up only when the entire entity stack can fit in those five usable slots. If it cannot fit, the item remains exactly where it is instead of entering locked inventory and being thrown back out.
- Unlit Twig Piles can be dismantled one Twig at a time.
  - Left-clicking an unlit pile retrieves one Twig.
  - Empty-hand right-clicking an unlit pile retrieves one Twig.
  - Right-clicking an unlit pile with a non-Twig item also retrieves one Twig.
  - Right-clicking with a Twig retains the existing add-to-pile/ignition behavior.
  - Lit Twig Piles cannot be dismantled.
  - If one Twig cannot fit in the primitive inventory, the pile remains unchanged and no item is dropped.
- Wild wolves automatically acquire survival players from 12 blocks instead of 24 blocks. Their other accepted behavior is unchanged.
- Locked inventory-slot shading now renders underneath the slot item and cursor/dragged item instead of visually covering item sprites.
- The ProjectFlint advancement path now continues through the existing fire progression with Kindling, First Flame, and Touched by Fire.

## Architecture introduced
`PrimitiveInventorySystem` now exposes `canFullyInsertIntoPrimitiveInventory`, a non-mutating capacity simulation for the five usable primitive slots. Interactions can therefore verify that a complete result fits before mutating the world.

`ItemEntityMixin` applies that rule at the head of vanilla world-item pickup. Vanilla pickup is allowed only when the complete item-entity stack fits in the primitive inventory; otherwise the pickup transaction is cancelled before any inventory mutation occurs.

Twig retrieval is centralized in `TwigPileBlock#takeOneTwig`, allowing left-click and right-click paths to share the same capacity-safe state mutation.

`ModAdvancements` provides a small server-side bridge for Project Flint processes that do not map cleanly to vanilla advancement triggers. It is used for physical milestones such as placing the first Twig Pile and successfully lighting one.

Locked-slot art moves to an `AbstractContainerScreen#renderSlot` HEAD injection. The disabled-slot background is drawn immediately before Minecraft draws that slot's item, guaranteeing item sprites and the later carried stack render above the lock treatment.

## Design reasoning
Loose surface objects are meant to be gathered with the player's environment interaction, not by forcing the active hand to be empty. Carrying capacity remains the actual constraint: if there is no legal space for the resulting Rock, the world object simply stays put.

The five-slot inventory must also be authoritative for automatic pickup. Letting vanilla briefly place overflow into locked slots and cleaning it up afterward created an emergent exploit where the player could repeatedly push extra items forward. Rejecting the pickup before mutation removes that loophole without deleting or relocating the world item.

Placed Twigs are an early physical construction resource, so placement should be reversible before ignition. Retrieval returns one Twig per interaction rather than breaking the whole pile into drops, preserving the deliberate physical-pile interaction and respecting the same primitive carrying limit.

Wild wolves should make approach dangerous without making merely entering the same forest effectively fatal. Halving only the automatic acquisition radius addresses the reported problem while preserving previously accepted wolf combat/navigation tuning.

Advancements are part of Project Flint's teaching layer. New technological capabilities can otherwise become opaque because vanilla recipes and advancement guidance have intentionally been removed. From this release onward, meaningful progression work must include an explicit advancement review in the same release rather than relying on a later cleanup pass.

## Tuning snapshot
- Wild-wolf automatic acquisition radius: 12 blocks (was 24).
- Primitive usable inventory slots: unchanged at 5.
- Twig retrieval: 1 Twig per interaction.
- Loose Rock/Rock stack limits: unchanged.
- Stone-working strikes/chance: unchanged at 8 strikes / 68% success.
- Grass and bush breaking timings: unchanged.
- Animal retaliation damage/reach: unchanged.
- Twig Pile capacity, ignition chance, ignition cost, and burn duration: unchanged.

## Assets and licensing
No textures, models, sounds, or third-party assets were added or changed. The existing temporary-asset ledger remains authoritative.

## Advancement coverage
This release retroactively covers the meaningful fire-progression capabilities introduced before advancement coverage became a release gate:

- `Kindling` — place the first Twig Pile.
- `First Flame` — successfully ignite a full Twig Pile.
- `Touched by Fire` — obtain a Lightly Burnt Twig.

`docs/DESIGN_PILLARS.md` now defines advancement guidance as part of self-teaching progression, and `docs/RELEASE_PROCESS.md` requires every progression-bearing release to document advancement coverage (or explain why no new milestone is appropriate). `docs/ROADMAP.md` also includes advancement coverage in stage-completion expectations.

## Validation performed
- Parsed all JSON resources using the Project Flint static validator.
- Verified Loose Rock gathering no longer checks for an empty held item.
- Verified Loose Rock world state is changed only after the Rock can fit in primitive storage.
- Verified the world-item pickup guard checks the complete item-entity stack before vanilla inventory mutation.
- Verified the primitive-capacity simulation considers only hotbar indices 0–4 and respects item max-stack sizes/components.
- Verified Twig retrieval is available from both block-attack and block-use paths, removes exactly one Twig, refuses lit piles, and leaves a full-inventory pile unchanged.
- Verified the wild-wolf acquisition radius is 12 blocks and no other wolf tuning constant was changed for this release.
- Verified locked-slot art renders at the head of per-slot rendering rather than in the final inventory-screen overlay.
- Verified the three new advancement JSON resources, parent chain, and server-awarded custom criteria.
- Verified the advancement documentation/release requirement is represented in the design pillars, release process, and roadmap.

## Known limitations
The artifact environment still cannot perform the final Fabric client launch. The local `build.bat` and client playtest remain the final compile/runtime gate.

The inventory tick cleanup remains as defensive protection for unexpected mod/command insertion into locked slots. Normal world-item pickup should no longer reach that cleanup path when the primitive inventory is full.

## Next pressure
Retest the opening specifically around full-inventory behavior: walk over item entities with all five primitive slots full, gather Loose Rocks while holding different items, and dismantle Twig Piles with both mouse buttons. Confirm wolves now create a close-range threat rather than acquiring the player from an excessive distance, and verify the fire milestones form a readable continuation of the ProjectFlint advancement page.
